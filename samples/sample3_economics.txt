Demand describes the quantity of a good that consumers are willing and able
to purchase at various prices during a given period. The demand curve slopes
downward because, for most goods, a lower price encourages greater
consumption. Supply reflects the decisions of producers, who are generally
willing to offer more for sale as the price rises. Where the two curves
intersect, the market reaches an equilibrium price and quantity.

Elasticity measures how strongly quantity responds to a change in price.
Necessities tend to have inelastic demand, because buyers cannot easily do
without them. Luxury goods, by contrast, often display elastic demand.
Governments consider these relationships when designing taxes and subsidies,
since intervention can shift either curve and alter the equilibrium.

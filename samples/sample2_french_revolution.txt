The French Revolution began in 1789 and transformed European politics within
a single decade. Financial crisis, unequal taxation and the influence of
Enlightenment ideas combined to undermine confidence in the monarchy. After
the Third Estate declared itself a National Assembly, popular uprisings
spread across the countryside. The storming of the Bastille became the most
famous symbol of these early events.

Historians still debate why the revolutionary government turned to violence
during the Terror. Some emphasise external war and internal rebellion, while
others point to factional competition in Paris. Napoleon's eventual seizure
of power did not erase the administrative reforms that the revolution had
introduced across France and, later, across much of Europe.

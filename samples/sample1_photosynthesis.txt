Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants convert light energy
into chemical energy. The reactions take place inside chloroplasts, which
contain the green pigment chlorophyll. During the light-dependent reactions,
water molecules are split and oxygen is released. The energy carriers ATP
and NADPH then drive the Calvin cycle, in which carbon dioxide is fixed into
glucose. Environmental factors such as light intensity, temperature and the
concentration of carbon dioxide all influence the rate at which glucose is
produced.

Plants that live in dry environments have evolved several adaptations. Their
leaves may be reduced to spines, and stomata often open only at night. These
features reduce water loss while still permitting carbon dioxide to enter
the leaf tissues for photosynthesis.
